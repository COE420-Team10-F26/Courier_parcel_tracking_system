# Initial Feasibility Study

**A. Technical Feasibility:** The team already knows the pieces this project needs, like a database for users, parcels, and status history, a backend to handle fully functional booking and role based access, and a frontend for three different types of users. None of that requires tools the team hasn't worked with already, so no research phase eats into build time. The only trickier part is role based access, making sure customers, field staff, and admins each see only what they are supposed to, but that's something the team understands rather than an idea to be invented. A shared repo and clear branching practices also mean 4 people can build separate increments without constantly stepping on each other's code.

**B. Economic Feasibility:** Building this system costs far less than buying off-the-shelf software, and it'll quickly pay for itself by eliminating costly mistakes.
- Lower Upfront and Running Costs: The main expense is the time it takes to build it. Hosting and database fees start small and only grow as the business grows, which is much cheaper than buying or licensing existing courier software.
- Fast Return on Investment: Replacing manual, untracked handoffs cuts out errors and reduces customer support requests. Once the system goes live, those savings will rapidly cover the initial build cost.

**C. Operational Feasibility:** The system replaces a manual, ask-around process with something all users can check for themselves. That's a low bar to clear for adoption since nobody has to change how they think about booking or delivery, they just get a screen to do it on. Field staff are the group that matters most here, they actually need to update statuses for the system to be useful, so that part of the interface has to be a couple of taps, not a long form, or people will skip it when they're busy. Admins already expect a dashboard, so that side is straightforward, and customers get a clear benefit, being able to track their own parcel without having to learn anything new.

**D. Schedule Feasibility:** The eight in-scope features split cleanly into increments, login first, then booking and tracking, then status and assignment updates, then modification and the dashboard, which fits a tight timeline with 4 people and weekly check-ins. The risk is the first increment slipping, since the schema and login is what everything else builds on.

**E. Overall Feasibility Conclusion:** Overall, the project is feasible within the semester. Technically, it doesn't call for anything the team hasn't used before. Economically, the cost is time rather than money. Operationally, it's a clear improvement on a manual process instead of a change users have to adjust to, and the schedule maps cleanly onto the increments the team already planned. The features left out of scope, payments, barcode generation, analytics, and dispute handling, are largely the ones that would have made each of the 4 areas harder, so keeping them out is part of what makes the rest of it achievable.
